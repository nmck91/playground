# Research Report: Educational Math Game Design for Siblings Ages 6-8

## Executive Summary

### Top 5 Must-Have Game Mechanics for Engagement

1. Adaptive difficulty systems
2. Decoupled progression
3. Fantasy/narrative context
4. Immediate visual/audio feedback
5. Collectibles & customization

### Reward System Structure
- Avoid extrinsic "if-then" rewards
- Use effort-based progression & mastery indicators
- Unlock narrative rewards and customization items

### Sibling Competition Approach
- Combine individual challenge and cooperative team goals
- Personal bests, role rotation, handicaps for fairness
- Celebrate effort, not just scores

### Critical Mistakes to Avoid
- Linking rewards directly to correct answers
- Fixed difficulty progression
- Visible failure tracking
- Sessions too long

---

## Detailed Analysis

### Engagement Mechanics
- **Fantasy-Based Math Battles:** Math in adventure/RPG style
- **Adaptive Difficulty:** Adjusts challenge by performance
- **Effort-Based Progression:** Unlocks from time/attempts, not accuracy alone
- **Immediate Feedback:** Satisfying visuals/audio for actions
- **Collectibles:** Customization options and pet companions

### Progression Systems
- Micro-levels (2 min), Macro-levels (chapters)
- Achievement systems focused on personal growth, not absolute performance
- Frequent unlocks: accessories, pets, themes
- Long-term unlocks: new characters, powers, worlds

### Competition Models
- Individual challenge mode (age-appropriate)
- Cooperative family quests
- Role rotation and handicapping for fairness
- Personal progress charts replace leaderboards

### Age Adaptation
- 6yo: Simple operations, encouragement, 10-minute sessions
- 7yo: More complex tasks, 12-15 min sessions
- 8yo: Advanced operations, multi-step tasks, 15-20 min
- Adaptive content and complexity

## Example Progression/Reward Structure
- Session 1: Tutorial, choose character, first accessories
- Session 5: Adaptive math, unlock themes/pet
- Week 1: New character, story chapter, parent feedback
- Month: Legendary pet, secret world, family quest mode

---

## Design Mistakes to Avoid
- Extrinsic rewards linked to correctness
- Fixed difficulty curves
- Public failure tracking
- Overwhelming younger children
- Poor UX/polish
- Aggressive monetization
- Ignoring parent needs
- Direct sibling age comparison

---

## Research-Backed Frameworks
- **Self-Determination Theory**: Autonomy, competence, relatedness
- **Flow Theory**: Challenge matches skill, clear goals, feedback
- **Growth Mindset**: Praise effort & improvement, not just ability

## Final Recommendations
- Make sibling play core
- Technical planning for Angular/Nx and mobile
- Decouple achievement from game progression
- Effort-based rewards, adaptive systems, focus on fun
